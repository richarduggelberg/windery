Wind speed data: Open-Meteo historical archive (ERA5) for Stockholm, used as a proxy for Swedish wind conditions. Solar irradiance data: Open-Meteo historical archive (ERA5) shortwave radiation for Stockholm, used as a proxy for Swedish solar conditions, converted to output via a simple flat-plate PV model (capacity factor = irradiance / 1000 W/m² STC rating). Demand is a synthetic, illustrative profile (not real metered data) scaled to Sweden's nationwide electricity use (~140 TWh/year) with realistic daily/weekly/seasonal shape. Slider defaults approximate Sweden's installed capacity as of 2025/2026: wind ~19 GW, solar ~4.8 GW, nuclear ~7 GW, hydro ~16.5 GW, no coal or gas capacity, and no grid-scale batteries (battery storage is still an emerging technology in Sweden — the slider lets you explore adding large-scale storage, and the coal/gas sliders let you explore adding dispatchable fossil capacity that Sweden currently lacks). Demand is met additively, in priority order: base capacity (nuclear + coal combined) generates a constant output at all times, wind and solar add on top of that, the battery discharges to cover any remaining deficit, then hydro, then gas (both last resort), dispatching only what wind, solar, base, and the battery couldn't, each freely up to its own installed capacity. Only surplus base + wind + solar generation charges the battery — hydro/gas never charge it. The chart shades the gap between base + wind + solar generation and demand red where they fall short (battery/hydro/gas had to help) and green where there's a surplus (charging the battery or exporting). Battery is modeled with unlimited charge/discharge power and a simple energy cap.

Illustrative, editable price curve — different from the capital build costs above and from nuclear/coal/ wind/solar's own fuel costs (which only feed the operating-cost table, not the price). Each hour's price is read off a continuous curve running through the anchor points above, positioned by how much of the hydro/gas/import capacity stack that hour's deficit (or surplus/curtailment) actually uses — so price swings smoothly with weather and demand instead of jumping between a few fixed numbers, matching how real prices move.
