Wind speed data: Open-Meteo historical archive (ERA5) for Stockholm, used as a proxy for Swedish wind conditions. Demand is a synthetic, illustrative profile (not real metered data) scaled to Sweden's nationwide electricity use (~140 TWh/year) with realistic daily/weekly/seasonal shape. Slider defaults approximate Sweden's installed capacity as of 2025/2026: wind ~19 GW, nuclear ~7 GW, hydro ~16.5 GW, and no grid-scale batteries (battery storage is still an emerging technology in Sweden — the slider lets you explore adding large-scale storage). Demand is met additively, in priority order: base capacity generates a constant output at all times, wind adds on top of that, the battery discharges to cover any remaining deficit, and variable (gas/hydro) capacity is the last resort, dispatching only what wind, base, and the battery couldn't. Only surplus base + wind generation charges the battery — variable capacity never charges it. The chart shades the gap between base + wind generation and demand red where they fall short (battery/variable had to help) and green where there's a surplus (charging the battery or exporting). Battery is modeled with unlimited charge/discharge power and a simple energy cap.

Levelized figures (LCOE/LCOS) fold in lifetime capital, operating, and financing costs per unit of installed capacity; wind's figure is weighted up to reflect the extra grid/network buildout it typically requires compared to plants sited near existing strong grid infrastructure.
